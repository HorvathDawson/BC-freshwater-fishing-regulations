Which form, and why (our reading, not the data's)

A, the slot grid, is still the one to take forward for the quota — the day's total as that many fixed slots, every tier and kind a span across the same slots, so nothing can read as 4 + 2 and the big number is never small. A size cap rides on the box as a dashed band with one token per big fish.

V, the Venn / Euler, is the one to put behind it. It answers the question A cannot: which limits does this fish count toward? Nested boxes carry "part of" (the river's 2 inside Region 5's 5, Bella Coola's "1 cutthroat or rainbow in all" inside the 2); dashed rings carry the caps that cut across boxes (only 1 over 50 cm; Bennett's "3 Dolly Varden and lake trout together", which crosses the lake's own box — a genuine Euler region, not a tree); and a kind sitting outside a box is the lift made visible (Vedder's hatchery rainbow in the river's 4 but outside the 2 from streams). The steelhead extra is the ring's tag ("1 over 50 cm · 2 if one is a hatchery steelhead") plus the +1 on its chip. Costs: it does not show how many slots are left, so it is not a replacement for A; and the rings need a measured overlay and room for their tags, which is why the boxes are generous. Suggested: A's slot rows inside V's boxes, one drawing.

K, the card deck, is the phone fallback. One tile per kind and size with its stamps is scannable at 390 px and complete; it only loses containment (you read that the 2 is inside the 4 from the stamps, you do not see it).

T, the decision tree, is right as a wizard, wrong as a picture. One fish at a time it is the clearest of all ("over 50 cm → hatchery → keep, 2, = steelhead, counts toward the 4, the 2 and the 1 over 50"); drawn whole it repeats "wild go back" fourteen times and runs three screens on a phone.

F, the flow, is a desktop explainer for one group, not a card. It shows one thing better than anything else here — six kinds funnelling into 2 slots while the rainbow has its own lane to the 4, and the caps as hoops the ribbons pass through — but it needs a sideways scroll on a phone and the single-kind groups (15 whitefish → 15) are dead weight.

J, the jars, is fun and no more. Jar widths say "the 2 is half the 4" nicely and the fish are a delight, but the labels crowd under the fish, the rings fight the metaphor, and it says nothing V does not.

B, size bands, keeps its job — this fish, this long: may I keep it? — and is still the only drawing where Bennett's lake trout window (short or very long, not in between) is obvious without reading. C mostly restates the strip; the weekday row and the part strip are worth keeping.

Tried and dropped earlier: nested circles (areas do not read as counts, and two tiers of 4 and 4 draw as one circle) and a caps-by-kinds matrix (complete, unreadable at phone width). The Venn here avoids both by drawing containment as boxes and only the cross-cutting caps as rings.
