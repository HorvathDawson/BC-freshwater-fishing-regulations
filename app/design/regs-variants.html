Three Ways to Read a River

The same water, the same day, the same four rules — laid out three ways. Everything below is rendered from what the bundle actually holds today. Pick one, or pick parts.

What the current screen shows, and why it is not enough

Harrison River renders two rows — “Stretch 1 of 2” and “Stretch 2 of 2” — both CLOSED, both captioned closure · gear restriction · vessel restriction. They look identical. Checked against the bundle, they nearly are:

setwhat it coversrules bound
352the river itself — 6 reaches, blue line 356362488 3, all written for the Harrison
265two short side channels at the Chehalis mouth the same 3, plus one more — reached from the Chehalis, in force 1–31 May only

So on 7 September the two are genuinely identical, and no amount of colour can separate them. Only naming the rules can. Three further facts drive the designs: the rows are in backwards order (the 0.9 km of side channel sorts before the 17.7 km river, because section ids sort by blue line first); the header’s “3 written rules” omits the Chehalis rule, the one thing that makes this river interesting; and the red CLOSED bar is a dusk-to-dawn closure — the river is open every daylight hour of the year, and the schema has no way to say so.

Variant A

Stretch cards, rules inside

Each stretch is a card named by its endpoints, with its rules on the face of it. The second card states only what is added. Closest to how American Whitewater and DriveBC present a linear feature.

What every variant fixes, regardless of which you pick

The headline is what the rule says, not what kind it is. details is populated on all 3,050 rules, averages 24 characters, and is already written for a human — “Dead fish bait allowed only when fishing for sturgeon”. It appeared nowhere on the old screen; kind was the headline and rendered as a raw enum.

A stretch is named, not numbered. The extent already carries landmark labels — location is real geography on 737 rules, and section_down resolves each end to the water it meets: 356362488:0 flows to the Fraser, and 356362488:84128 to Harrison Lake. “Fraser River confluence → Harrison Lake” is derivable today.

A rule that arrives by the tributary walk says so. 98.6% of bindings province-wide are via: trib, so a badge on every one is wallpaper — but the 2,921 sections carrying both kinds are exactly where it distinguishes one stretch from its neighbour, and Harrison is one of them.

A rule out of force is shown, greyed, with its dates — not filtered out. Filtering is right for the outcome and wrong for the listing; it is precisely why the two rows collapsed into twins.
