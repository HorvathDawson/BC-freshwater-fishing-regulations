### 1. Zone and provincial tables (the base rules) are pinned only to hand-typed transcriptions, and the test skips their quota numbers
Severity 4, missing-check, confirmed, reaches an angler; area regs-parse.
- Evidence: Water rows end at the extractor's text (ingest_catalogue.py:91 takes regs_verbatim from the batch). The 122 `z...` entries are hand-authored; their only outside check is pipeline/tests/test_catalogue_entries.py:158-183, which (a) skips any quote under 25 characters (line 178), (b) checks rules only, never licensing quotes, (c) compares against data/curated/regulations/reference/*.md, itself typed by hand. Nothing compares those .md files with the PDF except the p.80 definitions (pipeline/common/synopsis_definitions.py). The number guard (validate_catalogue.py:464-475) only needs the number inside the rule's own, author-written quote.
- Example: Measured on the corpus: 99 zone rule quotes are skipped by the 25-character rule, 91 of them carry a `take` (z1:daily_quotas trout_quota.r1 "Trout: 4", z1:hg_quota "Kokanee: 10", "1 over 50 cm"). 41 of 122 zone regs_verbatim are not a contiguous run of any transcription (stitched). 8 of 47 zone licensing quotes are not in the transcriptions (zp:basic_licence, zp:classified_waters_licence). I found no wrong value; the point is that none would be caught.
- Second opinion: Numbers are exact. One softening the finder left out: all 99 skipped short quotes ARE present in the transcriptions today (0 missing when I ran the same substring test on them), so the skip hides nothing now; a corpus-wide substring test on 'Trout: 4' would be near-worthless anyway, which is why the fix must be per-region and on word boundaries. The real exposure is the one hop no code covers: reference/region-N.md versus the PDF. No wrong value was found by either of us.
- Proposed fix: 1) Check short quotes too, against the rule's own region file, on word boundaries. 2) Check licensing quotes. 3) Add a deterministic reader for the region pages' quota boxes (like synopsis_definitions.py for p.80) or at least a per-page check that every number and date range in reference/region-N.md is present in that PDF page's word list; run it in the fast tier and in the annual runbook.

Severity 3, missing-check, confirmed, reaches an angler; area regs-parse.

Only `dispatch --redo-invalid` (dispatch.py:193-218) counts never-returned rows.
- Example: Annual-book runbook step 7 (handoff/PLAN/runbooks/annual-synopsis.md) says "Every FAIL line is an entry left out". A changed row the model skipped has no FAIL line; in step 8 it appears under `removed`, the list the owner is asked to confirm as gone from the book.

### 3. No standing check that the catalogue's stored row text still equals the extraction's
Severity 3, missing-check, confirmed, reaches an angler; area regs-parse.
- Evidence: regs_verbatim is copied from the batch once, at ingest (ingest_catalogue.py:91). After that the region files and data/generated/regs/extraction/synopsis_raw_data.json are independent; the review app and hand edits can change regs_verbatim, and an extractor change can move raw_regs. `run_parse.sh parse` exports with --skip-existing (run_parse.sh:74), which skips by id, so a changed row with a surviving id is never re-parsed (the runbook warns of this for the annual update only). pipeline/tests/test_catalogue_entries.py:34-47 relies on the ingest-time copy as the custody for all 1,393 water rows.
- Example: Measured now: 1,393 rows, 1,393 matching entry ids, 0 page mismatches, and exactly 2 entries whose text differs from the extraction: r2:chilliwack_vedder_rivers...@2-4 and r2:coquihalla_river@2-17 (both known hand fixes of extraction defects). So it is clean today, but only by measurement.
- Second opinion: Nothing material. Line is 89, not 91. Clean today by measurement; the gap is that a later hand or app edit to regs_verbatim that changes a printed number or date, together with its rule, passes every gate because all quote and number checks compare against the edited text. The extraction JSON is a 532 KB generated file, so the proposed test needs the needs_source marker like its neighbour.
- Proposed fix: A fast test: for every water row, squash(regs_verbatim) == squash(raw_regs) of the row with the same entry id, with a named allowlist (the two entries above, each with its reason). About 20 lines; needs only the extraction JSON.

### 4. A re-parse can only keep every edited entry or overwrite every one; the ledger is 7 weeks stale and the README tells you to re-seed it
Severity 3, fragility, confirmed; area regs-parse.
- Evidence: ingest_catalogue.py:196-204: an entry is replaced whole (`merged[e.entry_id] = e`) unless its digest differs from ingested.json; `--replace-edited` lifts that for every entry in the run, with no per-entry choice and no look at review marks. The ledger is gitignored (data/.gitignore:40) and dated 2026-09-23. pipeline/regs/parsing/README.md (Safety notes) says to run `seed-ledger` before the next repass. pipeline/regs/review/fingerprint.py:3 refers to an ingest `--replace-entry` (T135, DONE); no such flag exists anywhere in pipeline/ or curation-review/backend.
- Example: Measured now: ledger 1,510 ids; of 1,515 entries only 9 are unchanged since the seed, 1,482 edited, 24 never seen, 19 ledger ids no longer exist. A repass today KEEPs nearly everything; after the documented re-seed, a repass of a flagged entry silently replaces the curated copy, including curator-only fields the parser never writes (tributary_excludes, cut_pick, undrawn_part, anadromous_rainbow, hand-fixed regs_verbatim on Coquihalla and Chilliwack/Vedder).
- Second opinion: Two overstatements. (1) The README (parsing/README.md:101-103) says to re-seed only after the work dir was deleted, not as a routine step; the routine re-seed is in the annual runbook (annual-synopsis.md:183), run after adopting the new book, which is the right moment. (2) '1,482 edited' is not all owner curation: format migrations and repair passes (lengths, when, gear) also change the digest. The core holds: the guard is all-or-nothing, and the decided per-entry replace (PLAN.md:179) is recorded as done but absent. No data is wrong today; the parse work dir is empty, so nothing can be ingested until a new export.
- Proposed fix: Give ingest a per-entry `--replace <entry_id>` (and drop the bulk flag or make it list and confirm). On replace, carry the placement fields (fingerprint.PLACEMENT_FIELDS) from the disk copy unless told otherwise, and refuse when the entry has an owner mark unless named. Change the README line: re-seeding is not a routine step. Remove or implement the `--replace-entry` reference.

### 5. Two value guards are looser than their comments: numbers match as substrings, and a date on a sentence that prints none is not tied to its line
Severity 2, fragility, confirmed; area regs-parse.
- Evidence: validate_catalogue.py:467-472: `printed not in rule.verbatim` is a plain substring test, so take 1 passes on "Oct 16", "10" or "15 cm"; lengths pass on any date digit. catalogue_parts/checks.py:84-86: `_own_dates_carried` returns None when the rule's sentence prints no range, whatever `when` the rule carries; the row-level checks (checks.py:222-229, 246-265) only require each printed range to be carried by some rule and police the `;` case, so a season can sit on the wrong sibling.
- Example: z7a:daily_quotas trout_char_quota.r5: `take: 1`, quote "NOTE: Bull trout (Dolly Varden) may only be retained Oct 16-Aug 14. ... only 30-50 cm in length." The quote states no 1; the check passes on the 1 in "16"/"14". (The value is right: the table prints "1 bull trout" in another line.) Dates: 68 rules carry dates their own sentence does not print; 14 of those have the date on a different printed line. I read all 14 and they are correct.
- Second opinion: Number part fully reproduced. For dates I confirmed the code path but did not re-count the '68 rules / 14 on another line' figures; take those as the finder's. Impact is as the finder states: corpus is clean, this is hardening for the next re-parse or review-app save (the same gate runs on a save), not a live wrong answer. Size checks were not separately measured; `lengths` bounds use the same substring test, so the same looseness applies.
- Proposed fix: Number check: match on digit boundaries (`(?<![\d.])N(?!\d)`), and for sizes require `N cm` or a range `A-B cm`; fix the one z7a quote to include "1 bull trout (Dolly Varden)". Dates: when a dated rule's sentence prints no range, require the range on the same printed line or the line the quote continues onto, else an advisory.

### 7. Several copies of the quote normaliser and of the entry digest
Severity 1, duplication, partly; area regs-parse.
- Evidence: Normalisers: catalogue_parts/text.py:34 `squash` (the gate's); pipeline/regs/review/coverage.py:30 `_folded` (a restatement, pinned to squash by a corpus test in test_review_screen.py); pipeline/tests/test_catalogue_entries.py:117 `_norm` (also folds curly quotes and spacing round dashes, which squash does not); extraction/extract_synopsis.py:50-51 (en/em dash only). Digests of an entry: ingest_catalogue.py:171 `digest` (full SHA-256, default separators), io.py:341 `_content_digest` (16 hex, compact), review/fingerprint.py:67 `digest` (16 hex, compact, `default=str`), batch_exporter.py:41 `compute_rows_digest`.
- Example: A zone quote with a curly apostrophe passes `_norm` against the transcription but would fail `squash` against a straight-quote regs_verbatim, and the reverse for the gate; the three entry digests of one unchanged entry are three different strings, so none can be compared with another.
- Second opinion: The copies exist as listed, but this is weaker than stated. extract_synopsis.py's function folds nbsp, dashes and curly quotes, not 'en/em dash only', and it is an extraction clean-up, not a quote comparer. _norm and squash are never applied to the same pair of texts (one is zone quote vs transcription, the other rule quote vs regs_verbatim), so the curly-apostrophe example cannot produce a disagreement today. The three entry digests answer three different questions (ledger, build vintage, review marks) and are never compared with each other; rows_digest hashes rows, not entries. Real residue: _norm should build on squash, and the ingest ledger could use io.entry_digest. Clean-up, no data risk.
- Proposed fix: Make `_norm` call squash plus its two extra folds; have ingest's ledger use io.entry_digest (re-seed once). No behaviour change otherwise.
