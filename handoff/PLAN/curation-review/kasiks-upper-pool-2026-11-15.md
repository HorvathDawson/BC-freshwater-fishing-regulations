# Kasiks River: where is "the Upper Pool"?

Research date 2026-11-15. Labels: **STATED** = written in a source (quoted). **INFERRED** = my reading or measurement. **NOT FOUND** = searched for, nothing turned up.

Distances are "FWA km": measured along the FWA route for the Kasiks mainstem (BLUE_LINE_KEY 360879632, FWA code 400-094444, 41.39 km long), starting at the Skeena confluence at 54.2921 N, 129.4012 W, next to the Hwy 16 bridge at 54.29376, -129.4000. Older reports measure km on a different baseline (see section 1c).

## Bottom line

- No public source names "the Upper Pool" or says where it is (**NOT FOUND**). DFO's wording has not changed since at least 2009.
- The lower Kasiks has exactly one feature that ends navigation and fishing: a short canyon with falls and cascades, followed upstream by a 40 m step in elevation. In FWA terms it runs from km 17.6 to km 18.4, at about 54.354 N, 129.536 W. Several sources describe it (**STATED**):
  - the 1983 DFO catalogue: "impassable cascade type falls";
  - the Skeena Fisheries Commission (2007), citing the Resource Analysis Branch (1976): "Reach 3 is a 1 km long section with falls and cascades";
  - Eastwood (1981), cited by the same report: "navigable for 18.5 km";
  - FISS and RAB records place cascades and a 5 m rock obstacle there.
- **Best candidate (INFERRED, medium confidence):** the plunge pool at the foot of the main drop. On satellite imagery the drop is at about **54.3556 N, 129.5362 W (FWA km 17.9)** and the pool is just below it, about **54.3554 N, 129.5361 W (km ~17.87)**. This is the top of the jet-boat reach and the upper end of the "highly productive" spawning gravel "below the falls".
- **The boundary signs (INFERRED):** most likely at the canyon mouth, where the river widens onto a gravel fan, roughly km 17.45 to 17.75 (about 54.352 to 54.354 N, 129.535 to 129.536 W). A right-bank tributary joins the Kasiks at km 17.45 (54.3518, -129.5350), which would make a natural sign spot.

### 1c. Why the old reports say 22.5 km and the map says 18 km (INFERRED)

- The FWA profile has no step at all at 22.5 km. It is flat from 18.4 to 23 km.
- The FISS coders placed the 1983 catalogue's "22.5 km" falls at FWA km 18.3.
- RAB (1976) puts Reach 3 (falls and cascades) at about 16 to 17 km. Eastwood (1981) gives navigation to 18.5 km.
- So the "barrier falls at 22.5 km" (catalogue and Bustard 1994c) and "Reach 3" are almost certainly the same canyon, measured from a different mouth or along a different channel. The catalogue's own figures disagree with each other (a length of 22.5 km, but spawning to 25 km).
- Alternative reading: the 22.5 km falls are a separate feature next to the order-5 tributary at FWA km 22.37 (a possible Huckleberry Creek). If so, the gravel bed "two km section below the falls" would be FWA km 20.4 to 22.4. But no elevation step, FWA obstruction or FISS obstacle exists there, so I rate this low.

## 2. Candidate positions for the Upper Pool

**A. Plunge pool at the foot of the canyon falls. FWA km ~17.85–17.9; 54.3555 N, 129.5361 W. Confidence: MEDIUM.**
- For:
  - It is the only falls/canyon in the lower river, documented by 1983 DFO, RAB 1976, FISS and FWA.
  - It is the upper limit of boating (navigable to 18.5 km) and of coho (fish concentrate "below the falls").
  - "Upper Pool" reads naturally as the last, uppermost pool anglers reach.
  - DFO's boundary sits below this pool, which protects coho stacked at the barrier. That is a standard DFO pattern: closures at the foot of falls where fish hold.
  - Imagery shows a distinct dark pool directly below the lowest drop.
- Against:
  - No source uses the name.
  - Kilometre figures differ between sources.
  - From imagery alone I cannot confirm that a jet boat can reach this pool rather than stopping at the fan around km 17.6.

**B. The big pool or run at the canyon mouth and tributary confluence. FWA km 17.45–17.6; 54.3518–54.3530 N, 129.5350–129.5360 W. Confidence: LOW–MEDIUM.**
- For:
  - This is where the deep, boatable channel meets the canyon outflow and the order-4 tributary joins.
  - It is the realistic top of jet-boat water.
  - Guides might call it the "upper pool", with a "lower pool" downstream.
- Against: if this pool is the Upper Pool, the signs below it would sit in plain meander water with no obvious landmark.

**C. A pool below the "barrier falls at 22.5 km", read literally, near the order-5 tributary at FWA km 22.37 (54.388, -129.541). Confidence: LOW.**
- For: the kilometre figure literally matches Bustard (1994c) and the 1983 catalogue.
- Against:
  - FWA shows no elevation step there.
  - There is no FISS obstacle there.
  - It lies above the canyon that limits navigation, so DFO is unlikely to have posted signs that anglers could not reach.

**D. Pools inside the canyon or above it (km 18.4 to 27). Confidence: VERY LOW.** Boats cannot reach them, and coho mostly hold below the falls.

## 3. Where the boundary signs "below the Upper Pool" would stand (INFERRED)

- **If candidate A:** the signs are on the bank at the tail-out of the plunge pool or at the canyon exit, roughly FWA km 17.6 to 17.8 (54.3530 to 54.3545 N, 129.536 W).
  - Working cut point: **54.3535 N, 129.5360 W (km ~17.65)**. Everything below this point is open Sep 1 to Oct 31; the canyon and everything above it are closed to coho.
  - In practice the closed reach would be only about 0.3 km of canyon plus the inaccessible river above, so the rule mainly protects fish staging below the falls.
- **If candidate B:** the signs are below the confluence pool, around km 17.2 to 17.4 (54.350 to 54.351 N, 129.532 to 129.534 W).
- Under either reading, the open coho water is essentially the whole lower river from the Hwy 16 bridge up to the canyon mouth, about 17.5 km. The boundary uncertainty is roughly ±0.5 km around **54.353 N, 129.536 W**. That is enough to cut the river for mapping, but not enough to tell an angler where to stand.

## 4. What would settle it

1. **DFO North Coast Area, Prince Rupert or Terrace field office (Fishery Management / Conservation & Protection, the Terrace office).** They post and maintain the signs, so they can give a GPS position or photo of the boundary signs. The variation order or Schedule behind the Region 6 table also has an internal description.
2. **DFO stock assessment (Prince Rupert):** Kasiks coho and pink escapement field notes and stream inspection logs (SIL / NuSEDS narratives). Survey crews often name pools; "Upper Pool" may come from these.
3. **Bustard, D. 1994c. Fisheries assessment of pipeline crossings in the Kasiks and Khyex Rivers. Prepared for Pacific Northern Gas Ltd.** This is the FISS source for Arden Creek and the source of the "Huckleberry Creek" and "barrier falls at 22.5 km" statements. It probably has a site map. I could not find it online; ask the BC Ministry library or EcoCat for a scan.
4. **Eastwood (1981)**, the navigability survey cited by SFC 2007, and **Resource Analysis Branch (1976)** Kasiks reach descriptions (Aquatic Biophysical Maps 1:50,000). The full citations are in SFC 2007's reference list. Not found online.
5. **Local guides** (Nicholas Dean Lodge, Terrace jet-boat guides) and **Kitsumkalum First Nation fisheries**. They would know the local pool names (for example, Upper versus Lower Pool). Not contacted, per the brief.
